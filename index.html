IThe first chapter

Beaune, Burgundy

15–17 AugustTwo nightsThe gentle start

The journey opens softly. Four hours from home, the walled wine town of Beaune is the perfect place to shake off the road — cobbled lanes, a ramparts walk, and the famous Hôtel-Dieu with its glittering diamond-tiled roof and a Flemish masterpiece hidden inside.

An afternoon belongs to the cellars: Beaune sits at the heart of the Côte d'Or, and a first glass of Burgundy is poured underground, where it has always been.

Where we rest
Hôtel Les Remparts

A four-star home in a 17th-century mansion against the old ramparts — rooms named for vineyards, a quiet courtyard, the old town at the door.

IIAn interlude

Saint-Émilion

17–18 AugustOne nightAmong the vines

One golden night, and worth every kilometre. Saint-Émilion is a medieval village carved from honey-coloured limestone, ringed by some of the most famous vineyards on earth — a UNESCO town where old stone and great wine are the same thing.

We arrive as the light turns, wander the steep lanes, and let the evening be slow.

Where we rest
On a wine estate — Le Relais de Franc Mayne

A night in the vines: the guest house of a Saint-Émilion Grand Cru Classé, a walk from the village, with a cellar tour and tasting of our own.

IIIThe heart of the trip

The Dordogne

18–23 AugustFive nights · SarlatCastles & cliffs

Five unhurried nights in the loveliest corner of the trip. Sarlat is one of France's finest medieval towns — golden stone, a market morning, lanes that ask to be wandered. From here the days fan out gently: clifftop fortresses facing one another across the river, villages clinging to the rock, prehistoric paintings, and a pilgrim sanctuary built into a cliff.

One evening, the gardens of Marqueyssac are lit by thousands of candles. There is a pool for the hot afternoons, and no day asks for more than one beautiful thing.

Where we rest
Hôtel La Hoirie

A 13th-century farmstead in a green park just outside Sarlat — a pool, deep quiet, and the medieval town a short drive away.

IVChâteau country

The Loire Valley

23–26 AugustThree nights · AmboiseKings & Leonardo

Northward into the valley of the kings. Amboise is a handsome town beneath its royal château, and the days here belong to the great houses of the Renaissance — Chenonceau arching over its river, the fantastical roofscape of Chambord, and Clos Lucé, where Leonardo da Vinci spent his last years.

One château a day, a tasting of Vouvray in between, and evenings by the floodlit walls above the Loire.

Where we rest
Le Manoir Saint-Thomas

A 12th-century manor in the old town — gardens, a pool, and breakfast included — the most-loved address in Amboise.

See the hotel ↗

VThe final chapter

Riquewihr, Alsace

26–28 AugustTwo nightsRest & the table we love

The trip ends where we always like it to — among the fairy-tale half-timbered villages of Alsace, two hours from home. These last two days ask for nothing: the spa, a wander through Riquewihr and its neighbours, a vineyard or a hilltop castle if we feel like it.

And the evening we look forward to all year — dinner at La Table du Gourmet, our table in Riquewihr.

Where we rest
Le Chambard, Kaysersberg

A five-star Relais & Châteaux with a true spa, in a beautiful wine village — the relaxing finish, a clear step up from years past.
