The journey,
day by day

Fourteen days, one castle or wonder at a time — everything we'll see and do, from the first drive to the road home.

✦   ✦   ✦

A relaxed pace — one main thing each day, never a checklist, with long afternoons left free. Here is the whole journey laid out in order, with the sights, the drives, and the table booked for each evening.

The shape of itThe route across France

A clockwise loop of about 1,700 km — from home near Zürich, down through Burgundy to the south-west, up the Loire, and home through Alsace. Tap a marker for the stop.

Part OneBurgundy

Saturday 12 September · Dällikon → Beaune

Setting off

An easy four-hour drive into Burgundy. We arrive in the walled wine town of Beaune by mid-afternoon, leave the car, and let the first day be nothing but a slow wander of the old town and its ramparts.

Drive ~4 h
Dinner — L'Écusson, with its vegetarian menu
2Sun 13
Sunday 13 September · Beaune

The Hospices & a first Burgundy

The morning belongs to the Hôtel-Dieu — Beaune's medieval charity hospital, its glazed-tile roof, and the Van der Weyden masterpiece inside. In the afternoon, a guided tasting in one of the town's underground cellars: a proper introduction to Burgundy.

Hospices de Beaune · ~€12 eachCellar wine tasting
Dinner — Le Bénaton

Monday 14 September · Beaune → Saint-Émilion

South to the vines

The long transfer west, broken by a good lunch on the road. We reach Saint-Émilion in the late afternoon and check in to our wine-estate stay, then wander the honey-stone medieval village as the light turns golden.

Drive ~5 h 30A night on a wine estate

4Tue 15
Tuesday 15 September · Saint-Émilion → Sarlat

Cellars & a church cut from rock

A morning cellar tour and tasting on the estate, then the village's extraordinary monolithic church — an entire church carved from the limestone beneath the streets. A short, easy drive brings us to Sarlat, our home for five nights.

Estate tour & tastingMonolithic churchDrive ~1 h 45
Dinner — Gueule & Gosier, Sarlat
5Wed 16
Wednesday 16 September · Sarlat & the river

Market morning, river villages

Sarlat's Wednesday market fills the medieval streets in the morning. The afternoon follows the Dordogne river to La Roque-Gageac, clinging to its cliff, and the clifftop bastide of Domme — with the option of a gentle gabarre boat trip on the water.

Sarlat market (Wed)La Roque-Gageac · DommeGabarre boat ~€8–10
Dinner — Le Présidial, in its garden
6Thu 17
Thursday 17 September · The Dordogne castles

Two facing fortresses

The day of castles: Beynac and Castelnaud, two great fortresses staring at each other across the valley. In the afternoon, the sculpted boxwood gardens of Marqueyssac, with their sweeping views over the Dordogne — a gentle, beautiful close to the day.

Beynac · €12.50Castelnaud · €13.60Marqueyssac gardens · €12.90
Dinner — Ô Moulin, Carsac (Michelin Bib Gourmand)
7Fri 18
Friday 18 September · The Vézère valley

Seventeen thousand years of art

A morning at Lascaux IV — the breathtaking re-creation of the painted caves, where Paleolithic bulls charge across the rock. The rest of the day unwinds gently along the prehistoric Vézère valley.

Lascaux IV · €23 eachPre-book timed tickets
Dinner — Le Bistro de l'Octroi
8Sat 19
Saturday 19 September · Rocamadour

The cliff sanctuary

A day trip to Rocamadour — a pilgrimage village stacked in tiers of chapels and ramparts up a sheer cliff face. We go early, ahead of the crowds, then drift back to Sarlat for a quiet afternoon.

Village free to enterArrive before 10:00
Dinner — back in Sarlat
Part ThreeThe Loire Valley
9Sun 20
Sunday 20 September · Sarlat → Amboise

North to the valley of kings

A four-hour drive north into the Loire. We settle into Amboise and spend the evening in its old town, the royal château floodlit on the cliff above the river.

Drive ~4 h
Dinner — Les Arpents
10Mon 21
Monday 21 September · Chenonceau

The château on the water

An early arrival at Chenonceau, the most beautiful of all the Loire châteaux, arching gracefully across the river Cher before the tour groups appear. The afternoon brings a tasting of crisp Vouvray white among the vines.

Chenonceau · €19–24 eachVouvray wine tasting
Dinner — La Brèche
11Tue 22
Tuesday 22 September · Amboise

Leonardo's last home

A walkable day in town: the royal Château d'Amboise, where Leonardo da Vinci is buried, and Clos Lucé, the rose-brick manor where he spent his final years, its gardens scattered with models of his machines. (Or we swap in grand Chambord and Blois, if we'd rather.)

Château d'Amboise · €15Clos Lucé · €19 (combo €28)
Dinner — Château de Pray (1 Michelin star)
